# 002. One template names every block of a reply

Decided: 2026-11-13

---

## The problem

Five rules each named a situation of their own: a failed premise, going through items, a changed big picture, drift and a fitting topic. With `[WHEN]` gone they needed a home. The owner and the agent also had no shared names for the parts of a reply.

---

## The choice

The shape of a reply is one template. It names each block, says what the block holds and says when it is due: a title, a failed premise, a brief, the lead, the parts by kind, a note and the ending.

The line `<definition>` holds how the agent reads the goal, the scope and what done means. It is due when the thing is not defined.

---

## Not chosen

2 other ways:

- **The tree "define, discuss, do"**: two of its three leaves said what the ending tree and the kinds of parts already say, so it left three days after it came in.
- **A paragraph inside a part**: when a paragraph was allowed in a trial, the reply turned into prose.

---

## Consequences

The template is there for order and not for a measured gain: in a trial the owner could not tell its replies from the ones before it. "Give me the options and your recommendation" now points at two lines of the rules.

A template can be filled like a form. In 11 trial replies the kinds were not forced, and one reply printed three block names as bold labels. On 2026-11-18 the owner read such labels in a comparison and does not count them as a fault. What went with the tree is the order across a whole task.
