# 003. A tree decides what one reply covers, and no rule sets a length

Decided: 2026-11-13

---

## The problem

Simple was the default and had a definition: "a few lines, no headings". To stay inside it, the model kept its verdicts and cut the reasons and the steps between them. The owner read those short replies as harder than longer ones, and met it again in a session on 0.1.16.

---

## The choice

A tree decides what one reply covers: one lead and its parts, or the main thing and a list of what is left. Simple is a branch of that tree: a really simple thing gets a minimal reply. It has no length and no test of its own.

---

## Not chosen

3 other ways:

- **Simple as the default with a length**: it gave 8 to 10 lines on a short chat question and cut the reasons.
- **A principle alone**: "write as simply as you can" left the same question at 32 lines.
- **A test of whether the reader would ask why or how after the lead**: the owner took it out, since Simple is a minimal reply and nothing more.

---

## Consequences

The rules set no length. In a trial on 2026-11-13 chat replies came out about twice as long as under 0.1.16, and the owner read the longer one as much easier. Whether replies grow long across all tasks is for the next benchmark to show.
