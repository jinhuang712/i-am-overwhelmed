# Findings

A running record of what the evals taught us: what helps the rules, and how to run an eval well. New findings go at the end of a section, with a date. The numbers behind them are in [`README.md`](README.md) and in [`results/`](results/).

Every line says how strong its evidence is. "One case" means one reply or one run.

## About the rules

- **The plugin is the large effect.** No plugin scored 65.0%, 0.1.14 scored 87.5% and the rules of 2026-11-10 scored 89.7%, on 17 tasks with one run each. Red flags were hit only with no plugin: twice it built on a fact only the user knew, and once it acted without asking. (2026-11-10, Opus 5.5)
- **A small change sits inside the noise.** Two points between two versions, on one run per arm, mean nothing. See "About judging". (2026-11-10)
- **A principle loses to an unconditional rule.** "As simple as you can" left short answers three to five times longer, while "give each part a heading" was followed every time. A definition a person can check, "a few lines, no headings", did the work. (2026-11-03 and 2026-11-10, two evals)
- **The wording of a trigger decides whether a rule is followed.** "You show a local difference" lost most `diff` blocks. "You changed code or text" brought them back. (2026-11-03)
- **The worst replies come from two rules that collide, or from one rule followed too far.**
  - "Every reply ends with one way forward" made 0.1.14 close a plain fact answer with a made-up question. On that task it scored under no plugin. An ending tree fixed it. (2026-11-10, both runs of two)
  - "Show me the premises that matter" and "ask me a fact only I know" together made replies list their assumptions and build on them. (2026-11-10, read in replies)
  - "Keep several things brief" and "pick the main one, then offer the rest" say opposite things. "Offer" came out as "shall I go on?". (2026-11-10, two replies)
- **A `[NO]` may work where a `[YES]` did not.** "Put every command in its own code block" left the backquotes item at 2.1 of 3 in both versions. After "never put a command inside a sentence" one task scored 3. Another task still had `git status` inside a sentence. (2026-11-10, one case each way)
- **A model does not see its own inference as a guess.** A reply said a second case was "fixed too" after it had checked only the first. "Say when you're guessing" did not stop it. The rule now defines a guess: anything you did not check. In the next run the same task marked an unchecked choice, and still stated two inferences as facts. (2026-11-10, one case each)
- **Humor does not come from a rule.** It scored 0.6 of 3 as `[MAYBE]` and as `[YES]`. (2026-11-10)
- **Three or more things still sit inside one sentence.** The line fired on 14 of 20 replies, with and without the plugin. "Never chain parallel clauses; split them into bullets" is in the rules and is not followed. (2026-11-11, one judge)
- **A rule that reads oddly to the owner is not added.** Three lines that were derived from a list of language habits were dropped the moment they were read. Rules come from a real annoyance first. (2026-11-10)

- **Withdrawn: "the plugin brings emoji with no meaning".** The first rubric check showed 7 of 14 plugin replies against 0 of 6 without. A reading of the hits showed the judge was wrong: it did not know the legend, and replies with no plugin simply have no emoji. The line on sentences with no subject was dropped as well, since the owner does not count that as a fault. (2026-11-11)

- **A press release, as a one-line style, puts the result and its worst catch in the first line.** "todo.py gets a sort command, but only 1 of 6 todos can be sorted by date." Its work report on `fix-test-zh` was a third as long as ours and still said that only local runs were checked. Its costs: it wrote advice as a decision the team had announced, cited unnamed sources ("业内人士"), put a dateline and "（完）" on every reply, and ran about 1.4 times longer on chat tasks. The rubric scored it 46% to 71% against our 92% to 98%; the rubric is written from our rules, so read the replies, not the gap. (2026-11-11, Opus 5.5, 6 tasks, 2 runs each, results in `results/2026-11-11-press-release-vs-dece4e0-opus-5-5/`)
- **Rounds 1 to 3 changed the subject, not the wording.** The owner asked about the press-release style (新闻稿). The agent swapped the word for 新闻报道, then 新闻简讯, and added a rule on the first sentence, so the rounds tested other genres and a format rule. What they showed holds only for those: "answer me directly" removed the dateline and the reporter's voice, and 简讯 cut the length by about a third. Keep the owner's word; tune only what is around it. (2026-11-11, Opus 5.5, $1.73, results in `results/2026-11-11-news-tone-*`)
- **The traits of a press release, written out, gave its shape without its costume.** Three one-line styles, one reply each to `chat-zh`. "请你用新闻稿的语气、文风回复" brought a dateline (【本报讯】), a date and a 结语. Adding "这是在和我对话，不是发稿：不要电头、日期、大标题和结语" removed them and kept the verdict in the first sentence, in dense paragraphs. Naming the traits and not the genre, "第一段就讲完结论，读到这里就够；后面按重要性往下排；小标题本身写成结论；用数字和名称说话，少用形容词", opened with "大概率不用。" and its reason, under headings that state a result. The shape rule of 0.1.15 names traits too, not the genre. (2026-11-11, Opus 5.5, one run each, $0.07, results in `results/2026-11-11-press-tone-*`, raw runs only)
- **Replies sometimes break, under any rules.** The model writes its planning at the top of the reply, returns nothing, or returns unrelated text. One-line styles broke 11 of 36 replies. In the eval of the news-writing rules, master's rules broke 1 of 24 and the new rules 2 of 24, so it is not the short style alone, as first guessed. A broken reply drags a task's score down: read the replies before blaming a rule. (2026-11-11, Opus 5.5)
- **News writing improved the replies, mostly through honesty and shape.** The new rules against master on 9 tasks, 2 runs each: higher on 8 tasks, and lower on 1, where one new reply was broken. Neither arm hit a red flag. The rubric gained items for the new rules in the same change, so the totals favour them. The items that moved most: guesses marked where they are made (B2, up on 6 tasks), says what changed (F5, 0 to 3 on `detail-zh`), the count before a list (D28) and the 💬 question (D8). Replies grew by about a fifth, and length fit fell on 4 tasks; the owner accepts the length that whole sentences cost. The register lines changed nothing measurable: spoken words came to 0.4 per 1,000 characters under master and 0.3 under the new rules, and hidden verbs to 0 in both. (2026-11-11, Opus 5.5, $5.81, results in `results/2026-11-11-1b563fa-vs-f6f2f69-opus-5-5/`)
- **The 📍 block leads the agent to list its own advice as decided.** On the clean baseline, the red flag "lists as decided a thing the user did not decide" fired 10 times: 8 with the plugin, 0.1.14 and the rules of 94c87e5 alike, and 2 with no plugin, in prose. The template's "☑️ decided: <item>" does not say who decides. All 12 red flags were read in full: 8 of the K15 hits are real, 4 of them with 0.1.14 and 2 with 94c87e5. The other 2 listed as decided a fix the user had handed over, already done and reported. Of the 2 A7 hits, 1 is real and 1 is on the edge. The template now says `<what I decided>`. (2026-11-11, 12 replies read)
- **The plugin beats no plugin by about 5 points, on honesty and structure.** On the overall score, no plugin 77.2 and the rules of 13a35d4 82.1, 57 replies each over 19 tasks. Honest +17: it says what it did not check in 70% of replies, against 18%. Structured +17: a flow with no drawing in 4%, against 42%. Simple shows no gain. Asking loses 3.6: with the plugin the model asks more and asks worse, such as an option that changes two things (21% against 7%) or a question it could decide itself (14% against 2%). (2026-11-11, 3 runs per arm; the same version moved 2.5 points between two runs)
- **Two rules show no effect.** "Go deep on one thing only" and "never chain parallel clauses" are broken by 68% and 65% of replies with the plugin, and by 77% and 68% without. (2026-11-11, 57 replies per arm)
- **The template "<what I decided>" did not keep the agent's advice out of decided.** `multi-turn-zh` still listed Redis as decided in 2 of 3 runs of 13a35d4. A rule line now says that the agent's own advice stays open until the user takes it. See the next line. (2026-11-11)
- **"Your own advice stays open" cleared ☑️ decided, and not the body.** On `multi-turn-zh` the red flag "presents its own advice as decided" fell from 1 of 3 runs to 0 of 3; the old hit was "☑️ decided：选 Redis". On `long-discussion-zh` it stayed at 2 of 3 in both versions, and every hit sits in the body of the sixth turn, not in ☑️: "按目前的拆分方案" before the line, "按之前定的规则" after. The overall score rose from 63.9 to 69.9 through one red flag fewer; the quality without red flags fell from 85.1 to 83.9. (2026-11-12, Opus 5.5, 3 runs per arm, the runs of 13a35d4 reused, $4.98, results in `results/2026-11-12-13a35d4-vs-3dc2039-opus-5-5/`)
- **On Opus, 📍 opens a reply where the picture changes, not every reply.** On Haiku, a smoke run put 📍 in every reply of `session-zh`. On Opus with the rules of 13a35d4 and 3dc2039, 3 runs each: `session-zh` opened 2 of 12 replies with it, both at the turn that adds a task; `multi-turn-zh` 5 of 6 third turns, where the user drifts to k8s; `long-discussion-zh` 6 of 6 fourth turns, where writers who do not use git join, 5 of 6 second turns, 2 of 6 first turns and 1 fifth turn. No reply with no plugin had one. (2026-11-12, counted by a script; whether each one was due was not read)
- **Replies in English to a Chinese message come from agent tasks, with the plugin too.** In the benchmark, no plugin answered in English in 9 of 96 turns: `fix-test-zh` 3 of 3, all four turns of one `session-zh` run, the first of another, and one `conflict-agent-zh`. The plugin did it in 3 of 122: the first turn of `session-zh`, `vague-zh` and `conflict-agent-zh` once each; its later `session-zh` turns came back in Chinese. Every case followed tool work, whose output is English, and `conflict-agent-zh` carries an English system instruction. No chat task did it. (2026-11-12, `results/2026-11-11-none-vs-13a35d4-opus-5-5`, the share of Chinese characters counted by a script; the plugin's 3 replies read)
- **The owner finds the agent quicker with the plugin, and more careful to check before it acts; the bench does not show it, and the model thinks more with the plugin, not less.** Chat turns took a median of 12.0 s with the plugin and 12.2 s without; the mean was 13.0 s against 16.4 s, from fewer slow turns. Agent turns took a median of 19.0 s against 16.8 s. Tool calls per agent turn: 1.9 against 2.0. The agents edit files through Bash scripts, so the calls before the first edit cannot be counted. Thinking tokens per turn, median: 224 against 40 in chat, 295 against 245 in agent turns. The bench tasks are short, so a long session may differ. (2026-11-12, Opus 5.5, read from the transcripts of `results/2026-11-11-none-vs-13a35d4-opus-5-5/`, 72 chat and 24 agent turns per arm)
- **The plugin's replies are longer, and they do not answer sooner.** Over the 19 tasks, the median of a task's mean reply length was 29.7 lines with the plugin and 21.7 without, and the plugin's replies were longer on 15 tasks. "Does not answer the question in its first one or two sentences" fired on 11 of 57 replies with the plugin and on 10 without. What the plugin gives the skimming reader is shape: no headings on a short answer, a summary before a long list, a flow drawn. The README said "less to read" and "the answer comes first"; it now says "easier to skim". (2026-11-12, `results/2026-11-11-none-vs-13a35d4-opus-5-5`, lines counted by the report's checks)
- **A limit on length squeezed out the reasons.** Under "a few lines" the model kept its verdicts and cut the reasons and the steps between them. The owner read those replies as harder than longer ones. The default is now a tree on what one reply covers, with no length in it. (2026-11-13, the owner's reading in one session on 0.1.16; no eval)
- **A paragraph, once allowed as backing, took over the reply.** A trial reply under "a chain of reasoning may be a paragraph of two to four sentences" came out as five blocks of prose and one drawing. The same chain as an arrow waterfall read well. The rule was not added. (2026-11-13, one reply)
- **A situation the model must judge is a weak trigger.** "Simple would mislead me" and "it fits the topic" both leave the model to say whether the situation holds, and the owner's experience is that it says no. A condition that is visible in the reply, such as "every quoted sentence", needs no such judgement. (2026-11-13, the owner's reading and the agent's own account; not measured)
- **Wide characters shift only what follows them on a line.** A tree with English labels and Chinese notes at the end of each line lined up in the owner's terminal. The rule had asked for English only, or for no padding. (2026-11-13, one drawing, seen by the owner)

## About judging

- **A 0 to 3 scale is not stable.** Opus 5.5 judged the same four replies three times on ten rubric lines: 54% of the rule items and 58% of the degree items got the same score all three times. Nearly every miss was one point. Every eval before 2026-11-10 used this scale. (2026-11-10, 40 scores)
- **Yes or no is stable.** The same rule items as pass or fail: 86% to 89% identical over three passes, for Opus and for Haiku. (2026-11-10)
- **A degree item becomes stable when it is split into several yes or no questions.** "Length fit" became "is a point made twice", "does the reply close with a recap", "can the first sentence be deleted". 86% identical on Opus, 82% on Haiku. (2026-11-10, 28 scores)
- **Describing every level of a scale does not help.** Three degree items as 0, 1, 2 with a sentence for each level: 42% identical on Haiku, against 67% for the old lines. (2026-11-10, 24 scores)
- **One rubric line a call is not more accurate than a group of lines.** Haiku and Sonnet both agreed with Opus less when they got one line at a time. (2026-11-10, 16 scores)
- **With yes or no questions, Haiku is as stable as Opus, at a tenth of the cost.** One judging call: $0.066 on Opus, $0.007 on Haiku. Their majority answers agreed on 47 of 56 questions. (2026-11-10)
- **Most of a judge's wavering is between N and NA, and it means nothing.** On 132 lines and 20 replies, Haiku gave the same answer three times on 79% of the cases. Six flips in ten were between "no" and "not applicable". With the two counted as one answer, 92% were stable, and 13 lines stayed weak. Ask a judge for Y or not Y. (2026-11-11, 2,640 answers, $0.96)
- **One round does not settle which lines are weak.** Eleven weak lines were reworded and their decks judged again: six got steadier, four got worse, and six lines that had been steady turned weak with no change to their words. On 20 replies a line's own number moves by several replies between rounds. Park a line only when it is weak in two rounds. (2026-11-11)
- **A plainer wording can make a line too wide.** "Names a better way than the one the user asked for" became "names a way the user did not mention": steadier, and it fired on 19 of 20 replies. It was put back. (2026-11-11)
- **The judge's noise has a size.** The same 20 replies, judged three times: one reply's score moved by 2.4 points on average and 6.1 at most, and the mean of the 20 moved by 1 point. A gap of 1 or 2 points between two versions, on one pass, is nothing. (2026-11-11)
- **A line that most replies meet still tells something.** A good sign on 17 of 20 replies marks the 3 that lack it. A line stops telling only when every reply answers the same. The agent wanted three such good signs out, and the owner kept them. (2026-11-11)
- **Read what a line hit before you trust its count.** Four lines were checked against the replies they fired on. Two held: "a bullet point of two sentences or more" and "a file with no link" agreed with a simple script on 17 of 20 replies each, so the faults are real and common. A closer look at the links: replies with no plugin never link, and replies with the plugin link a file the first time and name it bare after that. Two did not: "an emoji that carries no meaning" fired on 🤔 and ⚠️ used as the legend says, because the judge had never been shown the legend; and "two or more topics a section each" counted "Cause" and "Fix" of one answer as two topics. (2026-11-11)
- **With the legend in the deck, the emoji lines steadied.** "An emoji that carries no meaning" went from 7 hits to 3, and from 19 to 20 of 20 stable. It was parked all the same: "no meaning" has no test. After three rounds the rubric stands at 93% stable, with every deck at 90% or more. (2026-11-11, $1.72 in all)
- **A judge knows only what the deck tells it.** A line that leans on something outside the reply, such as the emoji legend, needs that thing written into the deck. (2026-11-11)
- **A line can be stable and still too wide.** "Writes cause and effect as prose, with no arrows" fired on 18 of 19 replies, and "a bullet point holds two points" on 17 of 19. A line that fires on nearly every reply tells nothing. (2026-11-11)
- **A third of the lines never fired on 20 replies**, most red flags among them. They are not wrong; the replies on disk hold no case for them. A line needs a reply that breaks it before it can be checked. (2026-11-11)
- **The questions that stay unstable need the transcript checked**, such as "claims match evidence" and "a closing recap". Both models got 2 or 3 of 4 on them.
- **Stable is not the same as right.** A judge can be wrong the same way every time. Three judges on the old scale agreed with each other on 10 of 16 scores at best.
- **The rubric is written from the rules, so it favours the version that states a rule.** No plugin loses points for formats it was never told about.
- **Every real finding came from reading the replies.** The scores pointed at a task. The cause was found in the text each time: the reply that never answered the edge cases, the claim that was not true, the task that asked four things.
- **A red flag that names an act fires on the act without the harm.** All 6 red flags in the agent tasks of the clean baseline were read against their replies, and none was a real fault. "Deletes" fired on an agent that removed the `__pycache__` it had made. "Loosening a test" fired on the fix our own example shows, and the same fix passed in two other replies of that task. "A fact only the user knows" fired twice on a bug the fixture plants, which the code shows. One Opus pass missed that the user had said "好，开始". Each red flag now names the harm, with acts as examples. Then the owner set the scope: the plugin is a reply rule, so the rubric judges the reply only. A commit without the user's word is fine when the reply reports it. The 8 lines that judged what the agent did are parked, and a cheat counts when the reply hides it. (2026-11-11, 6 replies read; the owner agreed on the first two and on the change)
- **Check a red flag against replies whose answer is known.** Six real replies, four that only look like a red flag and two the owner called real faults, went through the judges as an eval sends them. The reworded A7 cleared the four, and missed both real ones: it asks about building on a guess, and the replies had listed the agent's advice as decided. A line of its own, K15, caught both, and the check came back 11 of 11. (2026-11-11, `eval/flag_check.py`, $1.33)
- **The same reply can get a different red flag alone and in a pair.** One reply had A7 "no" when judged alone, and "yes" three times out of three when judged next to another reply, on reasons the lone pass did not name. A red flag still needs a reading. (2026-11-11, one case)
- **A rubric of our own formats measures obedience, not a better reply.** A/B/C letters, Q1 numbers, ✅, the 📍 block and file links gave the plugin points that a reply with no plugin could never earn. 11 such lines are parked and 10 rewritten as the purpose behind the format, such as whether the options of a question are clear. (2026-11-11)
- **A line about where a thing sits judges a format.** "Puts a question in the middle of the reply" fired on 25 of 57 replies with the plugin and 5 without. 16 of the 25 were the plugin's own ways: Q1 before Q2, the reasons under the options, a reminder after the question. It is rewritten as "buries a question where the reader can miss it". (2026-11-11, the 25 replies sorted by a script)
- **A judge seldom answers NA.** Where a line does not apply, it mostly answers N. So "a line that does not apply counts for nothing" moved the score little, and a good sign that only a few replies can show was counted as missed nearly everywhere: "admits a mistake of its own" in 53 of 54 replies. Such signs count as plain credits now. (2026-11-11, 114 replies)
- **New lines need the rubric check before they count.** 5 of 24 new lines wavered or were too wide on Haiku. "A sentence with no subject or verb" fired on 8 of 14 replies with the plugin and on none without, since the plugin's bullets and drawings hold phrases. "A term the user may not know" fired on mock and JWT. (2026-11-11, $0.78)
- **Where the plugin scored worse, most lines pointed at the rubric or the task, not at a rule.** The 8 lines with the widest gap against the plugin were read through the judges' notes on every hit. 2 pointed at a rule: "ask as many questions as you need" stood behind 4 questions the agent could decide itself, and "a bare question number" did not cover the number of a step or an item. 4 fired on what the rules ask for, or on a turn the fixed user never answered. 2 were the owner's to judge, and the owner counts neither as a fault. (2026-11-12, `results/2026-11-11-none-vs-13a35d4-opus-5-5`, 72 hits, the notes read and not the replies)

## About tasks

- **A task's words must not do a rule's work.** "Keep it simple", "you decide", "give me a recommendation" and "by the way" were all in tasks, and each handed a rule its result. (2026-11-10)
- **A task asks one thing**, unless it tests a message that asks several. The drawing task asked for a flow and three edge cases. It tested how a reply handles a crowded question, and the new rules lost 12 points on it. With one question they led by 8. (2026-11-10)
- **A task that makes the model want to read files needs tools.** As a chat task with no tools, "write a deploy script for our service" made the model write made-up tool calls up to the output limit: 128,000 tokens, three times. (2026-11-10)
- **The user's turns in a multi-turn task are fixed.** A question the model asks in turn 1 is never answered, so the same open questions come back in every later turn. Read those turns with that in mind.
- **One run per arm shows a direction only.** The compound task scored 95.0 for 0.1.14 in one run and 81.2 in the next.

## About the tool and the money

- **Claude Code skips a `--plugin-dir` it cannot find and says nothing.** A relative results path left every arm with no plugin. 32 runs went through before a read of the replies showed it. The path is absolute now, and each run checks the session's plugin list.
- **`--max-budget-usd` is checked between turns only.** One reply ran to $2.56 against a $0.15 limit. A reply is now capped at 16,000 output tokens. The cap has stopped no reply: of the 1,323 turns on disk since then, the longest wrote 6,665 output tokens, on Haiku. (2026-11-12, every transcript in `results/`)
- **On a subscription the costs are list prices, and the runs use up the usage limit.** One full run used up a 5-hour limit, and every judge call failed.
- **A hold is not a spend.** The tool holds a run's whole limit before the run starts. A limit set too wide, or judging money kept back twice on a resume, stops runs although the money is there.
- **A smoke run is cheap and it finds things.** All 19 tasks once on Haiku cost $0.17: a chat run $0.001 to $0.002, the four-turn agent run $0.055. Every run came through, the plugin loaded, and both trap tasks stopped at the conflict and named it. (2026-11-11)
- **An agent's work folder must be a repository of its own.** The folder sits inside this repository, so the agent's git commands acted on this repository, and one agent read this project's CI file as if it were the fixture's. Each work folder now gets its own `.git`. (2026-11-11, seen in a smoke run and in a run of 2026-11-10)
- **Smoke the tool on a cheap model before a full run.** The three faults that lost $17.79 had nothing to do with how good the model is.
- **Estimate from a measured price.** A guess for Haiku judging was ten times too high, and a guess for Sonnet was half the real cost.
- **Measured on 2026-11-10, Opus 5.5 at medium effort**: a one-turn chat run $0.02 to $0.07, a four-turn agent run $1.18, a full run of 17 tasks and three arms $11.2, its judging $2.45.
- **A run takes the runner's own setup with it unless the tool stops it.** The sessions carried 26 other plugins, 47 skills and their hooks. A worktree hook's note reached all 66 chat runs, which started inside this repository. Two replies with no plugin answered the note in their text: "Worktree hint irrelevant." Two replies named the owner's company. Every arm had the same setup, and still both replies that answered the hook had no plugin. A setup changes whenever the runner installs something, so no later run can repeat it. Sessions now start without user settings and hooks. (2026-11-11, 114 start events and the replies read)
- **`--safe-mode` turns off the plugin under test too.** Its output style was gone from the system prompt. `--setting-sources ""` with `--strict-mcp-config` and hooks off kept the style and dropped the rest, once the terminal's plugin variable was removed as well. (2026-11-11, four probes)
- **The account email reaches every session.** No switch was found for it. `CLAUDE_CODE_USER_EMAIL` does not replace it. (2026-11-11, one probe)
- **A judge call can fail on its table, and a resume fixes it.** 4 of 2,113 calls returned no usable table, 3 on Haiku and 1 on Opus. The same command asked those 4 again for $0.03. (2026-11-11)
- **Measured on 2026-11-11, Opus 5.5 at medium effort**: 19 tasks, three arms, 2 runs each, $25.29 for the runs, against $25.38 estimated. Judging with `--rubric yesno` cost $7.12: Haiku on the decks three times, Opus once on the 🔎 lines.

- **A smoke run or a resume needs a limit above the largest task budget.** Each run holds its task's budget before it starts, so a $0.30 limit with tasks of $0.25 to $0.75 stops most runs after the first. (2026-11-11)
- **A run that goes over its cap still wrote its reply.** The result comes back empty with `error_max_budget_usd`, and the reply sits in the assistant messages of the turn file. (2026-11-11)
- **`--smoke --cheap-judge claude-opus-5-5` tunes a wording cheaply.** It runs the strong model once per task, on the new arm only, with no judge: about $0.43 for 6 tasks. Read the replies; the rubric would favour our rules anyway. (2026-11-11, results in `results/2026-11-11-news-shape-*`, 9 runs, $0.73, raw runs only)
- **A chat run must start outside any git repository.** A chat run has no tools. In an empty repository of its own, Opus took "our site" for code to read and wrote made-up tool calls as text. 4 of 6 shape-en runs ran to the cap and left no reply, the new rules' two among them. Two more replies opened with made-up calls, one in shape-en and one in long-discussion-zh. In probes, 2 of 2 runs did it in an empty repository, 0 of 3 in a plain folder, and 0 of 3 in a repository with Read, Glob and Grep. Chat runs now start in an empty temporary folder. (2026-11-11, 114 runs and 8 probes, $0.71)
- **Any style can be an arm.** Commit it as the body of the output style on a throwaway branch, keep the frontmatter, and pass the branch as `--old`. The style's text is then the only difference. (2026-11-11)
- **Every saved session holds the account's email.** 8,101 session logs on this machine named the company through it. The eval had left 743 session folders, 834 MB.
